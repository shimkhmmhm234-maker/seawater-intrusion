Why one metre of head moves the interface forty metres

Fresh water is about 2.5% lighter than seawater, so it floats on it. Balance the pressure where the two meet and the depth of the interface follows from the height of the water table.

z = h × ρf ÷ (ρs − ρf)

Here h is the water table above sea level, z is the interface depth below it, ρf is fresh water at 1.000 g/cm³ and ρs is seawater at about 1.025 g/cm³. With those values z is 40 times h. This is the Ghyben-Herzberg relation (Post, Houben and van Engelen 2018).

It assumes a sharp, static interface. Real aquifers have a mixing zone from metres to kilometres wide (Werner et al. 2013, USGS Circular 1262), and the ratio runs from about 1:33 to 1:50 as seawater density goes from 1.020 to 1.030.

The strip
About 40 km long and 6 to 12 km wide. The area is 365 km², or about 400 km² in some sources.
The aquifer
Kurkar sandstone and dune sand over Saqiye Group clays, 100 to 180 m thick in the west and 10 to 80 m in the east, split into four sub-aquifers.
Climate
Semi-arid. Rainfall falls from about 400 mm a year in the north to about 200 mm in the south.

Gaza's salt has four documented sources, and studies disagree on the weights

Not all salty groundwater is intrusion. Relict water, brines and dissolved evaporites also count (Werner et al. 2013). Select a source to highlight it.

Eastern inflow comes first

Weinthal et al. (2005) use boron, strontium and oxygen and hydrogen isotopes to argue that natural saline groundwater flowing in from the east is the primary source. Seawater is secondary and limited to the western margin.

Their data end around 2005.

Intrusion has grown since 2000

Mushtaha and Walraevens (2023) mapped chloride for 1972 to 2021. Before 2000 supply wells sat far from the sea. Afterwards some stood within 2 km of the coast, and intrusion followed.

They also map lateral inflow and upconing.

The two readings can both hold. They cover different periods, zones and sub-aquifers. No study splits the salinity between the four sources, so intrusion is best described as one process among several, likely dominant on the western margin and in deep sub-aquifers since about 2000.

How fast does the wedge move?

Sarsak and Almasri (2013) ran a SEAWAT model of northern Gaza with projected sea-level rise. They estimate seawater moving inland at about 65 m a year now, 35 m a year in a best case and about 80 m a year by 2035 in a low-recharge worst case.

What the Gaza case shows

Head sets depth

When pumping runs near three times recharge for decades and heads sit several to more than 15 m below sea level, nothing holds the sea back. Intrusion then depends on time and geometry.

Seawater is one source among several

Eastern inflow, deep brine and wastewater matter inland, and nitrate from sewage and fertiliser is a separate failure that protecting the aquifer from the sea would not fix.

Recovery is slow

The Netherlands shows lags over 100 years. Gaza scenario studies run 17 to 20 years, and UN outlooks say decades.

Unverified in the sources

The Palestinian Water Authority target of cutting abstraction from 180 to 70 MCM/yr by 2032 appears only in a secondary source. The "unusable by 2016, irreversible by 2020" projection comes from a news-service summary of a UN report. No source gives a quantified Gaza recovery time.
